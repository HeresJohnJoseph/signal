# THE DNA ESTABLISHMENT STRATEGY FRAMEWORK

## Purpose

A strategic development framework for category leaders who must protect what they have built, stay relevant as the world changes, and use authority to lead — not merely defend.

It is designed to ensure that an establishment strategy does not simply:

- restate heritage as current relevance,
- defend market share through spend,
- use size as a substitute for thought,
- or mistake familiarity for affinity.

Instead, it must identify:

1. **What the brand's authority is genuinely earned for — and what it no longer earns**
2. **How leadership becomes active, not passive**
3. **What cultural change the brand must acknowledge and absorb — before challengers use it against them**
4. **What the brand stands for beyond its market position**
5. **What new audiences or occasions must be earned before they are assumed**
6. **What creative behaviour signals genuine evolution without triggering abandonment by loyalists**

---

# THE STRATEGIC JOURNEY

## 1. DISTIL THE BRIEF

### Objective

Translate the brief from defending position into defining the next phase of category leadership.

Establishment brands do not need to create the market. They need to lead its next chapter without losing the authority that made them the leader.

### Step 1: Identify the leadership mandate

Frame the assignment as no more than three leadership acts:

Each act should begin with a stewardship verb:

- Renew…
- Lead…
- Earn…
- Reactivate…
- Defend…
- Expand into…
- Set the standard for…

### Template

**The client has asked us to:**

1. **Renew:** What about the brand's relevance must be actively refreshed — without abandoning the equity it has earned?
2. **Lead:** What category-level role must the brand now take on, beyond market share?
3. **Earn:** What new audience, occasion, or behaviour must be earned — rather than assumed?

### Questions to answer

- What has the brand earned through its history — and what part of that is still current?
- What has the category moved towards that this brand has not moved with?
- What do loyal customers love about this brand that it sometimes forgets to claim?
- What do lapsed customers say they want that the brand already has — but doesn't communicate?
- What does cultural leadership look like for this brand — and what would it require?

### Output

A concise leadership mandate:

*We have built the category. Now we lead its future.*

---

## 2. DEFINE THE STRATEGIC RENEWAL

### Objective

Identify the specific dimension of relevance that must be actively renewed — not through change of identity, but through re-activation of what the brand has always been capable of.

### Core question

> What has the brand been too comfortable to say — and what would change if it said it plainly?

### Template

**You asked us to:**

- [Leadership act 1]
- [Leadership act 2]
- [Leadership act 3]

**We asked:**

*How might this brand lead the category's future while remaining the reason the category exists?*

The renewal question should:

- identify what has been underused, muted, or forgotten about the brand's authority;
- connect current category pressures to a specific brand capability;
- create a role for the brand as category author, not category follower;
- contain a genuine point of view — not a reassertion of leadership for its own sake;
- give creativity a position to defend, not just a reputation to maintain.

### Weak renewal

*How do we remind people that we are the leader?*

### Stronger renewal

*How do we use our position to define what the category must now become — before someone else does it for us?*

### Establishment test

Ask:

- What has the category changed to that the brand has not acknowledged?
- What do people believe about the brand that was true five years ago but may not be true now?
- What position has the brand been too cautious to take — and at what cost?
- What would the brand do if it believed its position was genuinely threatened?
- What has the brand's scale prevented it from saying or doing that a challenger says freely?

### Output

One strategic renewal question.

---

## 3. ESTABLISH THE BRAND'S EARNED AUTHORITY

### Objective

Define precisely what the brand's authority is earned for — and where it has drifted, dimmed, or begun to be contested.

Authority is not the same as market share. It is the specific belief others hold about what this brand uniquely deserves to lead.

### Investigate four sources of earned authority

**A. Founding Purpose**

- What problem was this brand originally created to solve?
- What standard did it set that the category has since accepted as the norm?
- What did the brand once stand for that gave it cultural permission to lead?
- What belief or practice did this brand introduce that is now industry-wide?

**B. Accumulated Proof**

- What has the brand delivered — not claimed, but delivered — that others cannot match in record?
- What track record of performance, quality, or consistency has built the brand's authority?
- What do customers believe about this brand that they don't believe about anyone else?
- What has the brand done that would be impossible to replicate quickly?

**C. Cultural Significance**

- What role does this brand play in culture beyond its product?
- What occasions, rituals, or cultural moments does the brand own — genuinely, not just by presence?
- What does this brand signal about the people who choose it?
- What would be culturally missing if this brand disappeared?

**D. Present Tension**

- Where has the brand's authority drifted from its original basis?
- What does the brand claim that it no longer fully earns?
- What has the challenger's rise revealed about what the brand has stopped saying?
- What contradiction now exists between the brand's heritage and its current behaviour?

### Narrative structure

The brand was built to ______.
*It became the leader by ______.*
*It earned cultural authority through ______.*
*But as the category changed, the brand began to ______.*
*The opportunity is not to reinvent, but to reassert — on the terms that today requires.*

### Establishment question

Is the brand's authority still active — or has it become an assumption?

### Output

A short authority narrative:

- What we originally built
- What we proved that made us the leader
- What has changed
- What must be reasserted — and in what new form

## 4. NAME THE LEADERSHIP OBLIGATION

### Objective

Identify what the brand, as category leader, is now obligated to do — because no one else is positioned to do it.

Leadership obligation is not a defence of the past. It is the specific action that only a brand with this brand's scale, history, and trust can credibly take.

Often the strongest establishment strategies fulfill obligations of:

- category health (leading practices that make the whole category better trusted);
- cultural leadership (taking a position on what matters to customers beyond the product);
- standard-setting (defining what good looks like — so challengers have to respond to the brand's definition);
- access (using scale to serve audiences others cannot reach economically);
- institutional trust (being the brand that acts as the category's credible voice in public life);
- category honesty (being willing to acknowledge what the category gets wrong — before being forced to);
- innovation responsibility (leading the category's transition to new technologies, practices, or standards);
- audience investment (earning new generations without abandoning those who built the brand).

### Questions

- What does the category need that only the brand with the most to lose can credibly provide?
- What has the category been too fragmented, too competitive, or too cautious to address — and are we the brand that should address it?
- What do our customers need from us that goes beyond the product?
- What position in the culture can we take that no challenger is big enough to sustain?
- What would change in the category if we decided to lead it — rather than defend it?

### Leadership obligation articulation template

*As the brand that built this category, we have an obligation to ________.*
*This is not a marketing position. It is what leadership requires.*

Or:

*Others compete within the category.*
*We set the standard it competes towards.*

### Establishment discipline

Do not reduce the leadership obligation to:

- "being the best";
- "having the most history";
- "being responsible";
- "giving back";
- "being number one."

The obligation must be specific, active, and consequential — something the brand is uniquely positioned to do, and that the category or culture genuinely needs.

### Output

A single leadership obligation and a clear articulation of who benefits if the brand fulfils it.

# THE DNA DIAGNOSIS

## 5. DISTINCTION: FIND THE RENEWED DIFFERENCE

### Strategic role

For an establishment brand, Distinction is not the same as historical differentiation. It is the specific claim the brand must actively own in the present — not the claim it assumes is still owned by default.

The Distinction question:

> What do we own that we are no longer actively claiming — and what would it cost us if a challenger claimed it first?

### Diagnostic questions

**Dormant equity**

- What does this brand stand for that it has stopped saying?
- What historical association is still relevant but underused?
- What emotional or functional claim does the brand have clear permission for — but rarely deploys?
- What has the brand done or been that it has begun to take for granted?

**Challenged territory**

- What claim has a challenger begun to make that, historically, belonged to this brand?
- What cultural association has drifted — and needs to be actively reclaimed?
- What have customers begun to associate with challengers that should belong to the leader?
- What has the brand's scale, caution, or conservatism allowed to go undefended?

**Active authority**

- What would the brand's distinctive position look like if expressed with the same energy a challenger uses?
- What truth about this brand would surprise people if they heard it said plainly?
- What can this brand credibly own that is bigger than a product claim?
- What does this brand believe — and are we saying it?

### Distinction output

**What the brand owns by right:**
This brand has always stood for ________ — and still does.

**What has been underused:**
But we have stopped ________, which has allowed ________ to drift.

**The active renewal:**
We will reclaim ________ — and say it as clearly as we should have been saying it all along.

**The irreplaceable position:**
Only this brand can credibly say ________ — because only we have ________.

---

## 6. NUANCE: FIND THE CULTURAL EVOLUTION

### Strategic role

For an establishment brand, Nuance is not about discovering a new audience truth — it is about recognising how the truths of existing audiences have evolved, and how the brand must evolve to meet them.

The Nuance question:

> What have our most loyal customers come to need that we have been too comfortable to notice?

### Audience questions

**The loyal audience evolved**

- What do our most loyal customers believe now that they didn't believe five years ago?
- What expectations have changed — not just about this category, but about brands in general?
- What do they want the brand to say about the things that matter to them — not just the product?
- What would make them feel the brand still earns their loyalty, rather than assuming it?

**The lapsing audience**

- What do customers who have reduced their relationship with the brand say when asked why?
- What has the brand failed to evolve on that was important to them?
- What do they wish the brand would acknowledge?
- What is the gap between what they remember loving and what they experience now?

**The new audience**

- What does the next generation of this brand's potential audience need to believe before they trust it?
- What does the brand currently signal to them — and is it accurate?
- What would make this brand's history feel relevant, rather than dated, to someone encountering it for the first time?

### Nuance articulation

Move through four layers:

1. **Observation:** Our audience has changed in this way: ________.
2. **Tension:** They expect ________ from the brand they've been loyal to, but what they experience is ________.
3. **Emotional truth:** This makes them feel ________ — not disloyal, but ________.
4. **Cultural insight:** In a world where ________, loyalty cannot be inherited — it must be continually earned.

---

## 7. ADVENTURE: DEFINE THE LEADERSHIP MOVE

### Strategic role

For an establishment brand, Adventure is not disruption. It is the deliberate, consequential choice that proves the brand is still leading — rather than merely occupying the space it built.

The Adventure question:

> What will we do — not just say — that proves we are leading the category's future and not just protecting its past?

### Questions about boldness

**Positional courage**

- What truth about the category are we willing to state publicly that no challenger is positioned to say?
- What category issue are we going to lead on — not follow?
- What will we take a position on that makes our authority active rather than passive?
- What cultural conversation are we willing to enter — and what do we say when we're there?

**Behavioural proof**

- What change to our product, service, or experience will prove evolution rather than claim it?
- What investment — in people, in innovation, in community — will signal that leadership means something?
- What will we stop doing that no longer reflects what the brand stands for?
- What will we start doing that we have been too cautious to begin?

**Generational courage**

- What do we need to earn from a new generation — and what will that require us to change?
- What will we be willing to modernise — and what will we protect as non-negotiable?
- How do we signal evolution without triggering the perception that we've abandoned what made us trusted?
- What is the creative language of this brand's next chapter?

### Adventure articulation

*The brand that built this category now leads its next chapter.*
*We will ________ — because leadership is not a position. It is an action.*
*We believe ________.*

Or:

*Others in this category follow what changes.*
*We set the terms for what changes — and what does not.*

## 9. DEFINE THE STRATEGIC PIVOT

### Four strategic movements

**A. The Premise**
We believe that leadership is not a position — it is a continuous act of earning.

**B. The Promise**
We exist to ensure that ________ is always the category's most trusted, most relevant, and most forward-facing choice.

**C. The Position**
We are the brand that built ________ — and the brand that is now leading it forward.

**D. The Pivot**

From ______ to ______.

Establishment movement structure:

- From inherited authority to active leadership
- From assumed loyalty to earned loyalty
- From defending the past to defining the future
- From familiarity to renewed relevance
- From size as strength to wisdom as strength
- From category participant to category author

# THE ESTABLISHMENT OPERATING SYSTEM

## 10. SELECT THE TYPE OF ESTABLISHMENT BRAND

### Possible establishment roles

**The Category Author**
Takes responsibility for writing the category's next chapter — setting the terms for what the category becomes.

**The Trusted Standard**
Uses history and proof to embody what good looks like — making challenger claims feel provisional by comparison.

**The Cultural Institution**
Occupies a role in culture that goes beyond product — a brand people believe in, not just choose.

**The Evolving Classic**
Finds the version of what it has always been that is right for today — changing enough to remain relevant, protecting what cannot be changed.

**The Responsible Leader**
Uses scale to lead on the things that matter beyond market share — setting standards the industry eventually follows.

**The Intergenerational Brand**
Earns each new generation without abandoning those who built the brand's equity, by finding the shared truth that transcends era.

**The Authoritative Voice**
Uses its depth of knowledge to be the most credible, most honest, and most informed participant in category conversations.

### Selection questions

- What is the brand's authority actually earned for — and is it still current?
- What does cultural leadership look like for this brand — specifically?
- What do we need to earn from new audiences — and what do we need to protect for existing ones?
- What change is the brand willing to make — and what is non-negotiable?
- What does the category need from its leader — and are we providing it?

# FINAL QUALITY-CONTROL QUESTIONS

### Authority

- Is the brand's authority currently active or passive — and have we been honest about the difference?
- What has the brand been assuming is still true that needs to be re-earned?
- What has the challenger's rise revealed about what the brand has stopped doing?

### Brand

- Is the renewal grounded in genuine equity — or is it wishful repositioning?
- What must remain non-negotiable — and have we identified it clearly?

### Distinction

- What are we actively claiming today — not just historically?
- What have we allowed to drift into a challenger's hands — and how do we reclaim it?

### Nuance

- How has our core audience evolved — and are we meeting them where they are now?
- What does the next generation need to believe about this brand — and what do we need to show them?

### Adventure

- What is the leadership move — the action, not just the communication?
- What are we willing to change — and what does that change signal?
- Does the boldness feel like leadership or like desperation — and how do we ensure it's the former?

### Commercial Impact

- What specific behaviours must improve — loyalty, recruitment, reappraisal, consideration?
- What does category leadership deliver commercially that mere market share does not?
- What does the brand look like in five years if we get this right — and if we don't?
